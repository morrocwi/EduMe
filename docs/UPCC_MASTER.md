# 0. What This Document Is

This document consolidates the current UPCC architecture, the human-potential paper, the Toledo lineage, the global peer-review findings, and the empirical falsification programme into one standalone specification.

It is designed to answer five questions:

1. What is the mechanism by which a problem becomes a learning trajectory?
2. What language should actually be taught?
3. How does that language connect to tools, workflows, criteria, skills, practice, and the world?
4. How do we know that the human changed rather than the artifact merely improved?
5. How can every important node be removed or attacked experimentally to see whether it really matters?

UPCC does **not** claim a universal law of learning.

"Universal" denotes an intended compilation architecture that can be instantiated across domains while preserving context, governance, evidence, and human agency.

# 2. The Core Human-Potential Thesis

The unit of analysis is the **trajectory of a problem**, not the prompt, chat, lesson, or artifact.

The central question is:

> What must change in this person, through engagement with this problem or purpose, so that previously invisible, unreachable, unverifiable, or unactionable possibilities become responsibly reachable?

The final object of evaluation is not:

```text
the AI answer
the assignment
the course
the credential
the polished artifact
```

It is the **changed human agent** and the changed set of realistically reachable possibilities.

The paper expects change to become visible through traces such as:

```text
finer problem perception
larger repertoire of useful routes
stronger epistemic regulation
greater executable ability
retention under reduced decisive assistance
transfer
boundary / referral judgment
changed ability to act in the world
```

# 3. The Paper-Derived Mechanism: Problem → Word → Practice → Changed Human

The paper's central learning trajectory is:

```text
lived problem
→ current term
→ another domain's language
→ tools / workflows / criteria
→ skill gap
→ practice
→ world
→ revised premise structure
```

This is a **paper-derived trajectory**, not a new Toledo equation.

The deeper mechanism is:

```text
ORIGINATING EVENT / LIVED PROBLEM
        ↓
CURRENT PREMISE STRUCTURE
        ↓
CURRENT TERM / FRAMING
        ↓
ALTERNATIVE TERM OR ADDITIONAL LENS
        ↓
CHANGED QUESTIONS
        ↓
CHANGED DOMAIN ROUTES
        ↓
CHANGED TOOLS / WORKFLOWS / CRITERIA
        ↓
NEWLY VISIBLE SKILL GAP
        ↓
PRACTICE
        ↓
WORLD RESISTANCE / FEEDBACK
        ↓

        ↓
RETAINED KNOWLEDGE
        ↓
CHANGED HUMAN RETURN
        ↓
```

The originating facts can remain unchanged while the questions, routes, and available practice systems change.


# 4. The Most Important Clarification: What "Words" Must Be Taught?

The paper does not treat language as a glossary.

The valuable word is a **word that works on the problem**.

A word matters when it becomes an entrance into a **knowledge-and-practice system**.

Therefore the language layer of UPCC must teach not only the name of a phenomenon but the language required to **operate** within the route that the term opens.

## 4.1 Six classes of operational words

### A. Problem-junction words

These alter what body of knowledge and practice the problem can connect to.

Examples of function:

```text
name the phenomenon more precisely
open another domain
reveal another legitimate lens
surface a neglected constraint
expose a possible failure mode
```

Their purpose is to change the questions available to the learner.

---

### B. Tool words

These are the words needed to recognize, select, understand, and operate a tool.

They may include:

```text
tool name
component names
inputs
outputs
parameters
controls
variables
fields
commands
units
settings
states
error messages
```

A learner who knows only the problem-junction term but cannot understand the language of the tool has not yet crossed into practice.

---

### C. Workflow words

These are the words that describe how work actually moves.

They may include:

```text
stage names
sequence terms
handoffs
dependencies
preconditions
branch points
decision points
inputs / outputs
review points
approval points
escalation points
completion conditions
```

These words matter because a visible workflow is not yet an executable skill until the learner can navigate its sequence and conditions.

---

### D. Criteria and checking words

These are the words needed to decide whether an action, result, or claim is acceptable.

They may include:

```text
standard
threshold
criterion
evidence
validation
verification
comparison
tolerance
quality
error
failure mode
exception
scope
assumption
uncertainty
```

Without these words the learner may know how to produce an output but not how to judge it.

---

### E. Boundary, permission, and referral words

These tell the learner when **not** to act alone.

They may include:

```text
authorization
jurisdiction
credential
scope of practice
supervision
consent
permission
risk
irreversibility
referral
escalation
expert review
```

These terms are part of capability because recognizing a boundary can be the correct action.

---


### F. Skill and role words

These identify **who must do which part and what they must actually know how to do**.

They may include:

```text
skill name
role name
responsibility
scope
judgment point
required competence
supervision level
expert role
handoff role
review role
```

This class matters because the route from a problem to a tool or workflow eventually reaches a point where language alone stops carrying the work.

The learner must be able to distinguish:

```text
"I can name this skill"
from
"I can perform this skill"

"I can describe this role"
from
"I am authorized / competent to occupy this role"
```

Skill/role language therefore connects directly to:

```text
skill gaps
practice
expert roles
supervision
referral
increasing responsibility
```

This restores the sixth class in the paper's Table 2:
`Name of the skill or role → Who must do which part? → Skill gaps, practice, and expert roles`.

# 5. Operational Language Is the Bridge From Words to Skill

UPCC therefore treats "Working Words" as an **Operational Language Layer**.

A curriculum should not ask only:

> Can the learner define the term?

It should ask:

```text
Can the learner use the term to ask a better question?

Can the learner identify the tool it points toward?

Can the learner understand the tool's own vocabulary?

Can the learner follow the workflow?

Can the learner identify the relevant criteria?

Can the learner detect a failure mode?

Can the learner see what skill is still missing?

Can the learner know when to stop or refer?
```

A word is educationally valuable when it lowers the cost of entering a practice system **without pretending that entry equals expertise**.

This leads to a core implementation rule:

```text
Do not teach vocabulary as an endpoint.

Teach the minimum operational language required
to traverse the selected tool / workflow / checking route.
```

The phrase "minimum" is contextual, not universal.

# 7. Add a Lens, Do Not Replace Reality

The paper is explicit that a new frame should normally **add a lens rather than replace the lens**.

A new term can open a useful practice system while still becoming dangerous if it deletes:

```text
rights
power
legal risk
physical constraint
resource limits
safety conditions
institutional facts
other materially true features
```

Therefore every term-induced route must be checked in two directions:

```text
ROUTE OPENING
    What new knowledge / tool / workflow / criterion became visible?

FACT PRESERVATION
    What remains materially true from the originating problem?
```

A route that opens a real tool but deletes a critical fact is a routing failure.

## 9.1 Experiential Capital and Context Injection

**Source status:** derived from the paper's problem-framing logic and the companion practical proposal; this is a curriculum specialization, not a new Toledo equation.

`Life-Capital Context` describes surrounding conditions such as language, time, resources, mobility, mentoring, and credentials.

UPCC also needs to preserve **Experiential Capital**:

```text
what the learner has directly experienced
what has actually happened
what they have already tried
what failed
what partially worked
what consequences they have borne
what tacit distinctions they can already make
what local facts outsiders may not know
```

These are not interchangeable:

```text
Life-Capital Context
≠
Experiential Capital
```

The first concerns conditions around the person.

The second concerns what the person has learned from living with the problem.

### Context injection is a capability

The learner must be helped to state enough of the actual problem for downstream AI candidates to remain connected to reality.

A useful context-injection record asks:

```text
What happened?

What is directly observed?

What is inferred?

What has already been tried?

What constraints matter?

Who is involved?

What rights / power / permissions matter?

What consequences must be borne?

What is still unknown?
```

A vague problem statement can produce fluent output about the wrong or under-specified problem.

Therefore:

```text
problem articulation
is part of learning,
not merely a pre-processing step.
```

Context injection should be improved through questioning, comparison, and revision rather than by assuming the learner can fully describe the situation at the start.

# 12. Diagnostic Action

## REUSE — CAN-112 / CAN-170 family

When two explanations of learner difficulty remain plausible, choose a task that can discriminate between them.

Example:

```text
Hypothesis A:
    conceptual knowledge is missing

Hypothesis B:
    concept is present but tool fluency is missing
```

Do not repeat the same assessment.

Use a task whose expected observations differ under A and B.

The diagnostic goal is not to measure everything.

It is to reduce uncertainty that matters for the next educational decision.

## 15.1 Cheap Knowledge Must Stay Problem-Centered

**Source status:** companion practical proposal, absorbed into Candidate Routes / Knowledge-Like Candidates.

AI can generate candidate knowledge cheaply and abundantly.

That does not justify stockpiling it.

A candidate should remain in the curriculum only if it helps address a declared obstacle, clarifies a live route, improves checking, or reveals a justified next learning obligation.

Implementation invariant:

```text
candidate generation
without pressure from a live problem
tends toward accumulation rather than validation
```

Therefore:

```text
if candidate does not address the live problem,
does not improve a relevant route,
and does not reveal a justified check / skill / boundary:

    DISCARD or PARK OUTSIDE THE ACTIVE CURRICULUM
```

The aim is not maximal content.

It is to use cheap `K_like` for its legitimate role:

```text
dissolve a specific obstacle
to responsible human potential development.
```

## 17.1 Orientation in a Domain's Language Is Not Expertise

The paper draws a strict boundary between **orientation**, **interactional expertise**, and **contributory expertise**.

UPCC therefore preserves:

```text

≠
interactional expertise

describing a workflow
≠
contributory expertise

```

```text

tools
```

# 18. Language Barrier vs Knowledge Barrier vs Skill Barrier

A learner may:


These are different states.

## 21.1 Confirm-or-Challenge Gate

**Source status:** visible in the paper's Figure 2 branch point and operationalized in the companion proposal.

Before a query or checking cycle, ask:

```text
Am I asking AI to confirm
what I already believe?

or

Am I asking AI to challenge it?
```

These are different epistemic orientations.

A confirmation-framed query may be legitimate, but repeated confirmation without new resistance is diagnostic evidence of closure risk.

A challenge-framed query deliberately seeks:

```text
counterexample
missing assumption
strongest objection
alternative explanation
failure condition
discriminating evidence
```

This gate is not a replacement for independent verification.

It is a user-facing entry into DCP:

```text
Confirm
    may stabilize or clarify

Challenge
    seeks friction

Independent Verification
    supplies evidence that may actually change warrant
```

The compiler should flag prolonged runs of confirmation-only interaction when claim stakes or uncertainty warrant resistance.

## 25.1 From Procedure Visibility to Skill: Cognitive Apprenticeship

The paper explicitly uses cognitive apprenticeship to describe what must happen after a tool or workflow is found.

External instructional knowledge is absorbed **inside**:

```text
Adaptive Scaffold → Practice
```

rather than becoming a new architecture.

A practice sequence may use:

```text
modeling
→ coaching
→ scaffolding
→ articulation of reasoning
→ reflection
→ exploration
→ gradually increasing responsibility
```

### Modeling

An expert, teacher, practitioner, or AI-assisted demonstration makes hidden steps and judgment points visible.

### Coaching

The learner performs while receiving targeted feedback.

### Scaffolding

Support is supplied only where the current readout justifies it.

### Articulation

The learner explains:

```text
what they are doing
why this step comes now
what criterion they are using
what would make them change route
```

### Reflection

The learner compares their process with:

```text
expert process
alternative route
previous attempt
world feedback
```

### Exploration

The learner encounters changed cases and must make more of the route decisions themselves.

### Increasing responsibility

The learner progressively owns the parts of the work the curriculum intends them to hold.

This sequence remains adaptive rather than mandatory in every domain.

The important distinction is:

```text
visible procedure
≠
performed procedure
≠
judgment under real constraint
```

## 27.4 Rhythm Adaptation Gate

The system compares the **current readout** with the **next activity demand**.

It does not ask:

```text
"What is the learner's energy score?"
```

It asks:

```text
"Is this activity still adoptable and useful
for this learner / group at this moment?"
```

Possible adaptations include:

```text
KEEP
    continue because the current mode still works

SHORTEN
    preserve the objective but reduce the block length

SWITCH_MODE
    explanation → case
    reading → demonstration
    discussion → individual task

ADD_MOVEMENT
    standing sort
    gallery walk
    tool manipulation
    move to practice station
    physical workflow reconstruction

BREAK
    pause because continued demand is degrading performance or comfort

LOWER_DEMAND
    reduce simultaneous elements
    simplify language
    externalize steps
    provide scaffold

RAISE_CHALLENGE
    when disengagement appears to reflect under-challenge rather than overload

SOCIALIZE
    individual → pair / group interaction when socially useful

INDIVIDUALIZE
    group → individual work when group demand is obstructing learning

RETURN_TO_PRACTICE
    replace additional explanation with action / reattempt
```

No adaptation is universally preferred.

---

## 27.5 Movement Is a Learning Mode, Not Merely a Break

UPCC distinguishes:

```text
REST
from
MOVEMENT-IN-LEARNING
```

A learner may benefit from changing body position or location **without stopping the learning trajectory**.

Examples of abstract movement functions:

```text
physically sequence workflow elements
move between decision stations
manipulate the actual tool
inspect an environment
perform a demonstration
role-play a handoff
gallery-walk competing solutions
```

Therefore course design should not reduce movement to:

```text
"take a break every N minutes"
```

The more useful question is:

> Can the same capability obligation be continued through a different bodily / interactional mode?

## 27.7 Time Allocation Is Adaptive

UPCC therefore rejects a universal activity ratio such as:

```text
20 minutes lecture
10 minutes activity
5 minutes break
```

as a general law.

A course may begin with a planned time budget, but the delivery engine should preserve:

```text
capability obligation
+ learner / group readout
+ activity demand
+ time remaining
```

and adapt the route.

Thus:

```text
scheduled duration
≠
required learning duration
```

and:

```text
same total course time
≠
same activity proportions
```

Private, small-group, cohort, synchronous, asynchronous, and field formats may allocate time differently while serving the same architecture.

# 27.8 Delivery Modality Layer

UPCC does not assume that learning is face-to-face or synchronous.

The same capability architecture may be delivered through different modalities:

```text
FACE_TO_FACE
ONLINE_SYNCHRONOUS
ASYNC_SELF_PACED
ONE_WAY_MEDIA
BLENDED
FIELD / APPRENTICESHIP
HYBRID / MIXED
```

These are delivery conditions, not different theories of learning.

The compiler must preserve the same questions:

```text
What capability is intended?
What learner action is required?
What feedback is required?
What practice is required?
What verification is required?
What can be assessed in this modality?
What cannot be claimed from this modality alone?
```

Therefore:

```text
same content ≠ same learning function
same duration ≠ same opportunity for practice
watching ≠ doing
completion ≠ Human Return
```

# 27.10 One-Way Media Is a Valid Mode With a Narrower Claim

A course may consist mainly of:

```text
video clips
audio lessons
readings
slides
demonstrations
pre-recorded lectures
```

with little or no direct interaction.

This is a legitimate format.

But the system must distinguish:

```text
CONTENT EXPOSURE
from
LEARNER ACTION
from
PRACTICE
from
HUMAN RETURN
```

A one-way course is naturally strong at:

```text
orientation
explanation
demonstration
operational-language introduction
workflow visibility
worked examples
source navigation
```

It is naturally weak at directly observing:

```text
learner-specific barriers
guided correction
actual performance
boundary judgment
transfer
retained Human Return
```

unless compensating mechanisms are added.

Therefore:

```text

```

## 27.12 Self-Paced Rhythm

In asynchronous learning, the instructor cannot continuously observe learner state.

The rhythm layer therefore shifts partly to the learner.

The course should support:

```text
pause
resume
replay
skip already-mastered material where appropriate
change playback speed where appropriate
move from media to practice
take a break
return later
```

and should make transitions visible:

```text
WATCH
→ STOP
→ DO
→ CHECK
→ CONTINUE
```

This is preferable to treating a long video as one uninterrupted learning block.

No universal clip length is assumed.

The right segment length depends on:

```text
density
difficulty
novelty
language burden
need for practice
learner control
```

## 27.13 Asynchronous Activity Substitutes

When live interaction is absent, the compiler can substitute functions rather than imitate a classroom.

Examples:

```text

→ recorded explanation + worked example

live questioning
→ pause-and-answer prompt

teacher demonstration
→ recorded modeling + annotated steps

peer discussion
→ compare two contrasting cases / model answers

live coached attempt
→ guided practice file + staged hints

teacher correction
→ answer key / diagnostic feedback / worked repair

live challenge
→ adversarial case / counterexample / branching decision

live Human Return
→ submitted performance task / recorded demonstration / changed-case task

live referral
→ clearly visible expert / support route
```

A substitution is acceptable only when it preserves the intended learning function sufficiently.


Adds:


```text

```

Adds:




## 27.16 Blended Delivery

Blended learning should not mean:

```text
"some videos + some classroom time"
```

without function assignment.

A stronger design assigns each function to the medium that carries it best.

Example:

```text
BEFORE LIVE SESSION
    orientation
    operational vocabulary
    source navigation
    basic demonstration

LIVE SESSION
    diagnosis
    actual tool use
    coached practice
    challenge
    correction

AFTER LIVE SESSION
    reattempt
    transfer
    retained-knowledge record
```

Thus:

```text
media handles transmission
live time protects high-value interaction
```

when that fit is appropriate.

## 34.1 Retained Knowledge Is the Connector Between Cycles

The architecture does not end when a premise is revised.

A cycle must leave something with the human agent.

The paper defines retained knowledge broadly as:

```text
knowing what has a basis
knowing how to do something
knowing when it applies
knowing how to test it
knowing when to stop or refer
```

A compact post-cycle retention record is therefore:

```text
RETAINED_KNOWLEDGE_RECORD:
    what_has_a_basis
    what_i_can_now_do
    when_it_applies
    how_i_can_check_it
    where_it_fails_or_requires_referral
```

Retained knowledge changes the next cycle's:

```text
premise structure
vocabulary
known tools
workable workflows
checking criteria
practical skills
boundary judgment
```

Therefore:

```text
retained knowledge
becomes capital for the next cycle
```

The next problem is not entered by exactly the same human agent.

## 34.2 Institutional Access and the Visible-to-Actionable Gap

The paper's P9 extends the architecture beyond individual learning.

AI may make routes visible while access to:

```text
verification
practice
safe experimentation
qualified critique
expert referral
permission
institutional support
```

remains expensive or concentrated.

Therefore:

```text
visible option
≠
actionable option
```

UPCC treats institutional access as part of the conversion environment around:

```text
Human Return
→ World Feedback
→ Opportunity Conversion
```

An institution can increase responsible potential development by lowering the cost of:

```text
checking
practice
safe referral
supervision
access to appropriate tools / data / spaces
```

without lowering safety floors.

The curriculum should therefore record not only what the learner can see, but whether the required verification, practice, and referral infrastructure is reachable.

## 35.2 Minimal-Friction Course Closure

A learning architecture should not turn post-course support into repeated surveillance or administrative burden.

UPCC therefore distinguishes:

```text
COURSE CLOSURE
from
POST-COURSE RETURN
from
ON-DEMAND SUPPORT
```

### Course Closure

Everything the learner needs for ordinary continuation should be handed over **before the course closes**:

```text
retained-knowledge record
practice / tool support
source route
checking route
known boundary / referral route
next action
how to request help if needed
```

The course should not depend on several later messages merely to complete what should have been completed at closure.

### Single Return Window

**Status:** IMPLEMENTATION DEFAULT, not a universal learning law.

When delayed Human Return or first world use matters, schedule **one** return window after enough time has passed for the learner to use or test the learning.

The exact timing is domain-dependent.

The return checks only:

```text
USE
    What was actually used?

FRICTION
    What failed, was forgotten, or became unclear?

RETURN
    What can the learner now do / explain / check with less decisive support?

NEXT
    Continue?
    Practise?
    Learn next?
    Refer?
```

### Group and private use the same protocol

```text
PRIVATE
    one short individual return conversation / task

GROUP
    one group return session
    + individual return trace for each learner
```

Group discussion does not replace individual Human Return.

### After the Return Window

Default support becomes:

```text
SELF-SERVICE
+
ON-DEMAND HELP
```

rather than repeated proactive follow-up.

Additional scheduled follow-up is justified only when:

```text
the domain requires repeated supervised practice,
safety or governance requires monitoring,
the programme explicitly includes longitudinal development,
or the learner voluntarily opts into more support.
```

### Burden principle

This closure rule follows the existing burden discipline:

```text
support that is theoretically useful
can still be behaviorally excessive
```

The architecture therefore seeks:

```text
enough continuation support
without unnecessary intrusion or follow-up burden.
```

# 43. The Integrated Curriculum Compiler

```text
FUNCTION UPCC(problem_or_purpose, diagnostic_evidence, target, time):

    # 0. Preserve the originating problem
    P_raw = preserve(problem_or_purpose)

    # 1. Governance
    governance = apply_adult_or_child_governance()

    # 2. Finite Human Readout
    H = read_human(diagnostic_evidence)
    attach provenance, uncertainty, scope, contestability

    # 3. Diagnostic action if uncertainty affects routing
    while routing_relevant_uncertainty_is_high:
        run lowest-cost safe discriminating diagnostic
        update H only when evidence warrants

    # 4. Context
    K_life = read_life_capital_context(H, P_raw)

    # 4A. Delivery modality
    modality = select_delivery_modality(
        capability_target,
        learner_context,
        access,
        time,
        tool_requirements,
        interaction_requirements
    )

    modality_contract = build_modality_capability_record()

    # 5. Barrier readout
    B = read_barriers(P_raw, H, K_life)
    never infer observed difficulty == skill deficit

    # 6. Current framing and words
    W0 = capture_current_terms_and_questions(P_raw, H)

    # 7. Generate alternative additive lenses / problem-junction terms
    Wcand = generate_working_terms(
        preserve_originating_facts = TRUE
    )

    # 8. Route generation
    C_cand = generate_routes(
        problem = P_raw,
        words = Wcand,
        barriers = B,
        context = K_life,
        previous_return = H.previous_return
    )

    # 9. Route fit
    for route in C_cand:
        check:
            connection to actual problem
            assumptions
            preconditions
            critical facts preserved
            local fit
            safety / permission
            evidential status

    # 10. Human endorsement
    C_live = human_endorse_valid_routes(C_cand)

    # 11. Extract operational language
    for selected_route in C_live:

        WorkingWords = extract(
            problem_junction_terms,
            tool_terms,
            workflow_terms,
            criteria_terms,
            checking_terms,
            skill_role_terms,
            boundary_terms
        )

        # teach only what is needed to navigate the route
        operational_language_gap = compare_with(H)

        capability_obligations = derive_from(
            route,
            operational_language_gap,
            barriers,
            tool_requirements,
            workflow_requirements,
            checking_requirements
        )

        dependencies = build_route_specific_dependency_readout(
            capability_obligations,
            provenance_required = TRUE
        )

    # 12. Deployment
    deployment = select_by(
        Stakes,
        LearningNeed,
        Irreversibility,
        DependencyRisk,
        UserSkill
    )

    # 13. Candidate minimal sufficient scaffold
    scaffold = build_candidate_scaffold(
        barriers,
        operational_language_gap,
        tool_and_workflow_requirements,
        dependencies,
        deployment,
        time_remaining
    )

    # 14. DCP
    anchor()

    query_orientation = choose_confirm_or_challenge()

    expand()
    oppose()
    discriminate()
    verify()
    integrate()

    # 15. Practice
    practice(
        operational_language,
        tool,
        workflow,
        criteria,
        errors,
        boundaries
    )

    # 15A. Rhythm / burden adaptation
    state_readout = read_activity_state_without_inferring_latent_energy()

    activity_demand = read_activity_demand_profile()

    if current_mode_is_no_longer_adoptable_or_useful:
        adapt_rhythm(
            KEEP,
            SHORTEN,
            SWITCH_MODE,
            ADD_MOVEMENT,
            BREAK,
            LOWER_DEMAND,
            RAISE_CHALLENGE,
            SOCIALIZE,
            INDIVIDUALIZE,
            RETURN_TO_PRACTICE
        )

    preserve_capability_target()

    # 16. Social / teacher orchestration if required
    preserve individual and group readouts separately
    preserve HUMAN / AI / JOINT attribution

    # 17. Reduce decisive assistance
    remove_support_only_where_human_ownership_is_required()

    # 18. Human Return
    return_evidence = test(
        framing,
        operational_terms,
        tool_selection,
        workflow_navigation,
        verification,
        execution,
        error_detection,
        repair,
        boundaries
    )

    # 19. Claim → Task → Observation → Warrant
    build_curriculum_evidence_records(return_evidence)

    # 20. Novel transfer
    transfer_evidence = test_changed_case()

    # 21. Safe world encounter
    world_feedback = act_under_appropriate_risk_mode()

    # 22. Retain what survived checking, practice, and use
    retained = build_retained_knowledge_record(
        what_has_a_basis,
        what_i_can_now_do,
        when_it_applies,
        how_i_can_check_it,
        where_it_fails_or_requires_referral
    )

    # 23. Update premises / human state only to extent warranted
    H_next = revise_human_state(
        return_evidence,
        transfer_evidence,
        world_feedback
    )

    # 24. Net advancement
    record Gain, Loss, Transfer, Ownership, Burden,
           BarrierChange, OpportunityChange, Provenance, Warrant

    # 25. Institutional access / opportunity conversion
    institutional_access = read_access_to(
        verification,
        practice,
        safe_referral,
        permission,
        supervision
    )

    # 26. Opportunity conversion
    read what became realistically reachable

    # 27. Correction history / responsible participation trace
    update_correction_history()

    # 28. Continue / complete / replan / refer
    if target evidence sufficient and transfer supported:
        close_course_with(
            retained_knowledge,
            support_route,
            checking_route,
            referral_route,
            next_action
        )

        if delayed_return_is_relevant:
            schedule_ONE_return_window()

        after_return:
            support_mode = ON_DEMAND

        COMPLETE_WITH_TRACE

    if boundary requires expert / permission:
        REFER_WITH_TRACE

    if time exhausted:
        PARTIAL_WITH_TRACE

    else:
        reread the same or next problem with H_next
        repeat
```

# 44. Core Invariants

```text
1. Live problem or purpose before syllabus.

2. Problem ownership ≠ problem origination.

3. Lens before premature classification.

4. Learner readout ≠ whole learner.

5. Observed difficulty ≠ skill deficit.

6. Working word ≠ useful route.

7. Useful route ≠ learned capability.

8. Problem-junction vocabulary is not enough:
   operational tool / workflow / criteria language may also be required.

9. Knowing tool vocabulary ≠ operating the tool.

10. Seeing a workflow ≠ performing the workflow.

11. Mechanical success ≠ semantic validity.

12. Add a lens; do not erase materially true facts.

13. Candidate route ≠ endorsed route.

14. AI suggestion ≠ human goal ownership.

15. K_like ≠ validated knowledge.

16. Explanation ≠ verification.

17. Many agreeing models ≠ independent evidence.

18. Scaffold ≠ control.

19. Exposure ≠ retention ≠ improvement.

20. Assisted performance ≠ Human Return.

21. Group performance ≠ individual Human Return.

22. Credential ≠ capability.

23. Capability ≠ realized opportunity.

24. Continued AI use ≠ failure.

25. Decisive dependence matters where human ownership is intended.

26. Transfer must be tested.

27. Referral may be evidence of boundary judgment.

28. Claim strength ≤ evidence strength.

29. Every activity must trace to a barrier, route, workflow dependency,
    checking need, safety condition, or capability obligation.

30. Every strong learner claim requires an evidence record.

31. Textbook order ≠ universal prerequisite order.

32. A dependency edge requires provenance.

33. No universal novice-to-expert ladder is assumed.

34. World action must be scaled to stakes, irreversibility, skill,
    permission, and governance.

35. Context injection quality constrains downstream candidate quality.

36. Cheap candidate knowledge must remain tied to a live problem;
    irrelevant candidate accumulation is not learning.

37. Orientation in a domain's language ≠ interactional expertise ≠ contributory expertise.

38. Name of skill / role must connect to actual practice, expert roles,
    supervision, and scope where relevant.

39. Confirming interaction ≠ challenging interaction ≠ independent verification.

40. Retained knowledge is capital for the next cycle only to the extent
    that it survives checking, practice, and use.

41. Visible option ≠ actionable option when verification, practice,
    permission, or referral infrastructure is inaccessible.

42. Correction history ≠ universal trust score.

43. Observable restlessness / fatigue / boredom / attention loss
    must not be collapsed into one latent "energy" score.

44. Activity rhythm should adapt to learner/group readout and activity demand
    while preserving the capability target.

45. Movement may be part of learning, not merely a break.

46. Planned time allocation ≠ fixed delivery ratio.

47. Course closure should hand over ordinary continuation support before dismissal.

48. One scheduled Return Window is the default when delayed return matters;
    repeated proactive follow-up requires an explicit reason.

49. Delivery modality does not change the capability target,
    but it changes which learning functions and evidence can be supported directly.

50. Video / reading / audio completion ≠ Human Return.

51. One-way media may support orientation and modeling without supporting
    independent capability claims unless action and evidence are added.

52. A media segment should have a declared learning function and,
    where needed, a learner action before progression.

53. Blended design should assign functions to modalities rather than
    merely distribute content across media.

54. The next cycle begins with a changed human only to the extent
    supported by evidence.
```

# 49A. P1: Problem Ownership Kill Test

P1 tests whether meaningful learner agency changes how the learner remains with inquiry.

Use at least three conditions:

```text
A. meaningful agency:
   learner helps define aims, constraints, and success criteria

B. yoked framing:
   learner receives the finalized framing produced by a matched learner

C. fully pre-shaped:
   task, aims, and criteria are predefined
```

The yoked condition controls for the possibility that the agency condition simply produced a better task.

Primary outcomes:

```text
continued engagement with the same problem
reasoned reframing
ability to explain why criteria changed
context-injection quality
later Human Return
transfer
```

A strong falsifier is:

```text
meaningful agency adds no benefit
beyond task quality, contact time, or motivational novelty.
```

P1 should not be supported by satisfaction alone.

# 50. P2 Must Be Tested as a Cascade, Not as Text Diversity

The paper's P2 predicts that pivotal terms alter the route set.

UPCC decomposes the empirical mechanism without creating new propositions:

```text

P2-b
questions change routes

P2-c
routes expose tool / workflow / criteria language

P2-d
operational language exposes a real skill gap

P2-e
practice converts some of the route into Human Return
```

These are **test stages**, not new theoretical equations.

A term can succeed at P2-a and fail later.

```text

route divergence
operational-language uptake
practice conversion

transfer
```

# 50A. P3: Orientation in a Domain's Language vs Expertise

P3 must separate **orientation** from **performance**.

### Orientation test

Measure time / burden required to identify:

```text
useful vocabulary
credible sources
tool names
workflow structure
criteria
relevant practitioner / expert role
valid next question
```

### Expertise / contribution test

Measure separately:

```text
conversation with blinded practitioner
tool operation
workflow execution
judgment under domain constraint
error detection
boundary recognition
performance quality
```

The predicted pattern is asymmetric:

```text
AI may lower orientation cost
faster than it lowers the cost of genuine contributory expertise.
```

Support for P3 therefore does **not** require AI to produce expertise.

A hard failure occurs if the orientation advantage disappears under matched resources, or if the framework repeatedly mistakes vocabulary/workflow fluency for genuine performance.

The programme proceeds through:

## 62.1 Factorial Optimization Rules

Use factorial or fractional-factorial designs only when components are:

```text
ethically toggleable
operationally separable
implemented with fidelity
not logically required by another manipulated component
```

Candidate factors may include:

```text
visible candidate status
independent verification prompt
structured practice
withdrawal test
external feedback
operational-language support
```

Do not interpret a non-significant main effect as proof of irrelevance when a pre-registered interaction is central.

Do not perform unrestricted interaction fishing.

---

## 62.2 Micro-Randomized Scaffold Experiments

For low-risk repeated decisions, randomize safe scaffold options at eligible moments:

```text
no added support
hint
worked example
operational-language cue
tool demonstration
workflow map
procedural information
reflection prompt
feedback
```

Measure both:

```text
proximal next-attempt performance
and
later Human Return / dependence
```

A scaffold that improves the next response but worsens later ownership is not automatically beneficial.

---

## 62.3 SMART for Adaptive Decision Rules

After component screening, use Sequential Multiple Assignment Randomized Trials where appropriate.

Example:

```text
initial candidate scaffold
    ↓ observe response

adequate response:
    maintain vs fade support

inadequate response:
    intensify vs re-diagnose / reroute

uncertainty remains:
    independent check vs further safe practice
```

The target is the adaptive rule:

```text
which response
for which learner state
after which evidence
produces better later Human Return
with acceptable burden and safety?
```

A learned adaptive policy requires later confirmatory testing.

---

## 62.4 Assistance-Fading Shock Tests

To distinguish coupled performance from Human Return, use pre-registered perturbations such as:

```text
remove conversation history
change AI model
withhold AI at one decisive stage
replace full answer with tool-only access
change surface features
insert a novel error
delay the return task
change source format
```

Choose shocks that attack the capability the study claims became human-owned.

## 62.5 Statistical Discipline

Confirmatory analysis should preserve:

```text
intention-to-treat
multilevel structure when learners/tasks/classes/sites are nested
repeated-measures structure
effect sizes + uncertainty intervals
pre-specified interactions only
multiplicity control for co-primary outcomes
equivalence tests when asking whether a node adds meaningful value
attrition / missingness sensitivity
fidelity analysis separate from ITT
```

No post-hoc universal potential score is permitted.

---

## 62.6 Power Discipline

Do not choose sample size from convention.

For each confirmatory test:

```text
validate the outcome enough to estimate variance
define the smallest effect that justifies keeping the node
power the actual design for that effect
include expected attrition
power interactions only when theoretically required
```

Pilot studies estimate feasibility and variance.

They do not decide the final hypothesis after the fact.

---

## 62.7 Blinding and Independence

Where feasible:

```text
outcome raters blind to condition
artifact raters separate from Human Return raters
analysis scripts frozen before condition labels are revealed
adversarial-task ground truth established independently
high-stakes return assessed through independent routes
AI model / version / system prompt logged and frozen in confirmatory trials
```

Many model names do not automatically establish evidential independence.

Do not randomize:

```text

unsafe engineering execution
legal action without authority checks
child safety or privacy protections
unqualified high-risk procedures
```

Use:

```text

sandbox
synthetic cases
controlled assessor experiments
```

# 65. Falsification Decisions

Before unblinding confirmatory outcomes, define the smallest effect that would justify retaining a node.

Decision categories:

```text
SURVIVES
    meaningful benefit on the targeted Human Return / transfer outcome
    without unacceptable burden or harm

CONDITIONAL
    value exists only under identifiable conditions

MERGE
    node is useful but cannot be separated from a neighbouring node

REMOVE
    node adds complexity / burden without meaningful return

REVERSE
    ablated or alternative condition performs meaningfully better

FALSIFY PROPOSITION
    repeated failure under valid measurement, strong controls,
    good fidelity, and independent replication
```

# 66. Architecture-Level Kill Criteria

UPCC requires substantial reduction if strong replication finds that:

```text
artifact quality already captures Human Return and transfer;

independent verification adds no correction beyond correlated agreement;

verification + practice + world feedback + withdrawal
do not improve post-assistance Human Return;

barrier-sensitive routing adds no value over ordinary personalization;

operational language of tools / workflows adds no value where UPCC claims it should;

trajectory traces do not predict retention or transfer;

Opportunity Conversion adds no explanatory value beyond ordinary performance;

a substantially simpler architecture matches or exceeds UPCC.
```

UPCC must earn its complexity.
